## How This Mini-Book Can Help You

Earlier this year a family emergency put me in a hospital for a long stay. I watched doctors handle critical, near-death cases up close. <!-- source: theory-hospital-workflow.md -->What struck me, besides the emotional weight of the situation, was how coordinated and rapid their response was.

<!-- source: theory-hospital-workflow.md -->
Despite the intensity, every team member knew their role, communicated clearly, and worked with precision, much like a machine.

In tech we don't work like that. Under pressure we stumble, communication breaks down, three people debug the same thing while another problem goes unnoticed. We react instead of acting with purpose.

So, what if we took a page from the emergency room playbook?

Most tech teams operate like general practitioners: stable, routine, manageable. Then a system is on the verge of collapse, a high-severity incident lands at 2 AM, and generalist habits stop working. We call in specialists, sure. But what if the team itself could respond with the precision of an ER crew? We wouldn't need more people; we could train the ones we already have.

This mini-book maps the emergency room to the on-call rotation.

<!-- source: theory-hospital-workflow.md -->
This idea may take time to fully develop, but I believe it could change the way we work or at least, second best option, make my work simpler.
