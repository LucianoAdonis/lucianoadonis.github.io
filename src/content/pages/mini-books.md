Adding this to not forget what I'm defining as Minibooks.

Learning to play through time it's actually more simple than you think, but it requires being organized.

I've started writing long-form content more times than I can count. A full book on Confluence, a complete guide to interviews, a proper deep dive into OKRs.

Each time, somewhere between chapter three and chapter five, the thing I was writing about changed enough to make the earlier chapters feel stale. Or I changed. Or the scope was so large that finishing it would take longer than the topic stayed relevant.

Mini-books are the answer I landed on.

---

## What a Mini-Book Is

A blog post is too loose and tends to disappear into a feed, and a full book takes so long to write that it's already partially outdated by the time it's done.

A mini-book has enough structure to be useful as a reference and is short enough to actually get finished. Most of them are between three and seven chapters. Some are one. A few will probably never get past two.

<span data-review="new">What I want is</span> something complete enough to hand to someone and say "here, this covers the basics".

The "How This Mini-Book Can Help You" page is a quick introduction to trick the reader into reading, haha.

---

## Why Things Deprecate

Technical content has a shelf life. Confluence changes its interface, Jira renames features, and a workflow that was the right answer in 2022 is the wrong one in 2024. A 200-page book about a specific tool is <span data-review="new">probably</span> a liability: you either maintain it forever or you let it rot.

Shorter content <span data-review="new">seems to</span> rot more gracefully. A three-chapter guide can be updated one chapter at a time, and if something becomes outdated the useful parts still stand.

---

## Who This Is For

Primarily, me.

I come back to these pages when I've forgotten how something works, or when I need to explain it to someone quickly and don't want to rebuild the explanation from memory. Having it written down means I think it through properly once and benefit every time after.

But if someone else finds one of these useful, even one person, even once, that's worth more than leaving the knowledge inside my head where nobody else can reach it. Most of what ends up here came from someone else originally, so writing it down and making it findable is a way of passing it on.

---

## Why Not Just Blog Posts

Blog posts are formatted for discovery. They need hooks and SEO and a reason for someone to land on them from a search engine. Mini-books are formatted for use: they assume you're already here and want to get through the material efficiently.

The chapter structure also forces some clarity. When you have to split something into chapters, you have to decide what belongs in each one, and that surfaces gaps and redundancies a single long post hides.

---

## The Status Colors

Every mini-book has a status:

- **Green**: complete enough to share without disclaimers
- **Yellow**: draft, the ideas are there but the writing still needs work
- **Orange**: jottings, rough notes that could become a book someday
- **Red**: barely started, a title and a direction, not much else

Most things live at yellow. That's fine.
